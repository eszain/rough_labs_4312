# Glossary

## Public Transit Lost & Found System

---

This glossary defines domain-specific terms used throughout the project documentation. Terms are defined in the context of the TTC Public Transit Lost & Found System. General computing terms are excluded unless they carry a project-specific meaning.

| # | Term | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lost-Item Report** | A digital record submitted by a passenger describing an item they believe was left on a TTC vehicle or at a TTC station. Includes item category, description, approximate loss date/time, loss location, and passenger contact information. |
| 2 | **Found-Item Record** | A digital record created by a transit operator or Lost & Found staff documenting an item that has been physically recovered from a TTC vehicle, station, or handed in by a member of the public. |
| 3 | **Tracking Reference Code** | A unique alphanumeric identifier generated by the system and provided to a passenger upon successful submission of a lost-item report. Used by the passenger to check the current processing status of their claim without re-submitting. |
| 4 | **Claim** | A passenger's assertion that a specific found-item record corresponds to their lost property. A claim must be verified through the ownership verification process before the item is released. |
| 5 | **Ownership Verification** | The process by which Lost & Found staff confirm that a claimant is the legitimate owner of a found item. May involve reviewing identifying details, uploaded photographs, serial numbers, government-issued photo ID, or other evidence. |
| 6 | **Match Candidate** | A pairing of a lost-item report with a found-item record that the system's matching algorithm flags as potentially referring to the same item, based on shared attributes (category, description keywords, date/location proximity). |
| 7 | **Item Category** | A standardized classification label (e.g., electronics, personal documents, clothing, bags, keys) assigned to both lost-item reports and found-item records to facilitate search and matching. |
| 8 | **Intake** | The process by which a found item is logged into the system for the first time, typically by a transit operator at end-of-shift or by depot personnel. |
| 9 | **Disposition** | The final recorded outcome for a found item in the system. Dispositions include: returned to owner, donated, disposed of, transferred to police (for items of significant value or legal concern), or retained past the holding period. |
| 10 | **Bay Station** | The TTC's centralized Lost Articles Office, physically located at Bay Subway Station in Toronto. This is the single collection and pickup point for all found items across the TTC network. |
| 11 | **Retention Period** | The fixed duration (set by TTC policy) during which a found item is held at Bay Station before being eligible for final disposition. After the retention period expires, unclaimed items may be donated, disposed of, or otherwise handled per policy. |
| 12 | **Redacted Catalog** | A publicly browsable list of recently found items displayed with sensitive identifiers (e.g., serial numbers, full names, specific bag contents) automatically removed or masked to protect privacy while still allowing passengers to recognize their property. |
| 13 | **Chain of Custody** | The documented chronological trail recording every transfer, status change, or handling event for a found item — from initial intake through storage, verification, and final disposition. Maintained via the audit log. |
| 14 | **Audit Log** | An immutable record of system actions including which staff member created, updated, or changed the status of an item record, along with timestamps. Supports accountability and regulatory compliance. |
| 15 | **MFIPPA** | Municipal Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act — Ontario legislation governing how municipal institutions (including the TTC) collect, use, disclose, and retain personal information. The system must comply with MFIPPA data retention and purging requirements. |
| 16 | **Data Purging** | The automated process of anonymizing or permanently deleting personal contact and identity information from resolved reports after a defined retention window (e.g., 90 days), in compliance with MFIPPA. |
| 17 | **Streamlined Intake Form** | A simplified data-entry interface designed for transit operators, requiring only the minimum necessary fields (date, route/station, broad item category) to log a found item quickly without disrupting transit schedules. |
| 18 | **Processing State** | The current status of a found-item record within the system's workflow. States follow the lifecycle: Received → Cataloged → Matched (potential) → Claim Under Review → Verified & Ready for Pickup → Returned / Disposed. |
| 19 | **Sensitive Identifier** | Any piece of information that could uniquely identify a person or enable fraud if publicly disclosed — e.g., serial numbers, full names, document numbers, bag contents lists. These are automatically redacted in the public catalog. |
| 20 | **Intake Latency** | The delay between when a found item is physically recovered on a transit vehicle and when it is processed and cataloged at the central Bay Station office. Publicly communicated as 24–48 hours by the TTC. |
