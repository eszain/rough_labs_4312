# Lab 4 Preparation: Elicitation Plan and Simulation Rules

## Elicitation Goal

Determine how intake staff at central Lost & Found verify ownership before returning high-value and non-identifiable items, and identify what happens when two people claim the same item.

Sprint 0 requirements established a basic match-and-notify workflow, but left the actual handoff and dispute rules undefined. We need concrete details on what documentation staff accept, how they resolve conflicting claims, and what they do with unverified items before building the verification interface for Sprint 1.

## Draft Questions

1. When someone arrives at the desk to pick up a found item, what is the exact step-by-step process you follow before handing it over?
2. What specific proof do you ask for when someone claims an item with no obvious identifying mark, such as a black umbrella, a generic water bottle, or a winter jacket?
3. How do you verify ownership for locked electronic devices like phones and laptops when the claimant cannot immediately power them on?
4. What happens when two different people file a claim for the exact same item?
5. If a claimant knows the brand and location where an item was lost, but gives the wrong color or model, what do you do with the claim?
6. How long does an item stay in the active verification state before you mark it unclaimed or move it to long-term storage?
7. Who has the authority to approve exceptions, such as releasing an item to a friend or roommate on the owner's behalf?
8. What paper forms, logbooks, or software records do you fill out during the handoff, and what information must the claimant sign for?
9. When a claimant cannot produce satisfactory proof on the spot, what next steps or appeal options do you give them?
10. Have there been instances where an item was mistakenly handed to the wrong person, and how was that handled after the fact?

## Course Simulation Rules

Any evidence collected during this lab comes from simulated roleplay rather than direct interviews with actual York University facilities staff. Course policy requires all simulated findings to be labeled explicitly:

* Every finding, quote, or workflow note derived from this exercise must carry the `[SIMULATION]` prefix.
* Analyst inferences drawn from simulated answers must be identified as inferences, not as confirmed stakeholder statements.
* Unverified assumptions needed to complete a scenario must be listed separately as assumptions.
* In Sprint 1 documentation, simulated evidence cannot be presented as empirical field data or used to close customer requirements without team validation.
